Inside the harbour authority's budget fight

By Marcus Ellery

The harbour authority's board met for six hours on Tuesday and still could not agree on next year's budget. At the centre of the dispute is a plan to dredge the eastern channel, which supporters say is needed to keep larger fishing boats working from the port and critics describe as an expensive answer to a problem that may not last.

The dredging would cost an estimated four million over three years. The authority's reserves would cover about half of that, leaving the rest to be raised through higher mooring fees or a loan. Members of the fishing cooperative attended the meeting in large numbers, and several spoke during the public session about the difficulty of entering the harbour at low tide.

Board members who oppose the plan argue that silt levels have varied a great deal over the past twenty years and that a cheaper programme of maintenance dredging could be tried first. They also point out that mooring fees have already risen twice since the pandemic, and that leisure sailors, who make up most of the harbour's customers, would bear much of the cost.
